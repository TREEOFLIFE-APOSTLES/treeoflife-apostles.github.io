The Flower of Life

🌸 The Flower of Life

How it grows

  1. One circle. The beginning.
  2. Two circles: the vesica piscis. A second equal circle is centered on the first one's edge. (Visit the Vesica Piscis page.)
  3. Seven circles: the Seed of Life. Six circles are placed around the first, each centered where the circles before it cross.
  4. Nineteen circles: the Flower of Life. Twelve more circles continue the same rule, and the pattern is usually shown inside a bounding ring.
  5. The Tree of Life. The ten sephirot of the Kabbalistic Tree of Life, with their paths, are often drawn over this circle grid. Here they light up in honor of our house name.

Abydos, Egypt

Flower-of-life patterns are drawn in red ochre on granite in the Temple of Osiris at Abydos. The temple itself is very old, but these drawings are generally thought to have been added much later, possibly in the Ptolemaic era or after. Scholars don't agree on exactly when or why they were made.

Leonardo da Vinci

Leonardo da Vinci's notebooks, including the Codex Atlanticus, contain studies of overlapping circles in this family of patterns. He used them in his geometric explorations of how areas made by arcs of circles relate to each other.

An ancient pattern, a modern name

Overlapping-circle grids like this appear in many cultures and periods. The name “Flower of Life” is modern, though. It was popularized in the 20th century, especially through New Age writing in its later decades. Older sources just describe the drawings themselves, not the name.

For our house

One circle becomes two, two become seven, seven become nineteen, and the Tree of Life rests inside. Different branches, same tree. 🌸
