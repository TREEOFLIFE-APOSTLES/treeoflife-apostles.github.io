🎵 Motion Music · Play the Air

What it is

Move your pointer (or finger) over the starry stage and a glowing orb follows you, leaving a trail. Horizontal position is pitch — left is low, right is high. Vertical position is volume — top is louder, bottom is quiet. Volume is capped low on purpose. There is no camera on this page, by default or ever.

With continuous glide the pitch slides smoothly between notes, like the instrument below. With pentatonic snap the pitch lands on a five-note scale (A–C–D–E–G), so almost anything you wander through sounds gentle together.

The theremin

In 1920, Russian physicist Lev Termen (known in the West as Léon Theremin) invented the theremin: an instrument you play without touching it. One antenna senses how close your hand is and sets the pitch; another sets the volume, and the air itself becomes the keyboard. This page borrows that idea with a pointer instead of antennas — move to make music, like a theremin.
