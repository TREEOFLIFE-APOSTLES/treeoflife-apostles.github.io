Numbers That Grow

Arithmetic becoming geometry, harmony and form. Every picture here grows from one simple rule repeated.

1 · Stacking triangles

Triangular numbers Tn = n(n+1)/2: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15… Two neighbours always make a square, Tn−1 + Tn = n² (3 + 6 = 9). Two copies of the same triangle make an oblong rectangle n(n+1): 2, 6, 12, 20…

2 · Squares and cubes

Left: an n × n square of n² tiles (1, 4, 9, 16…). Right: an n × n × n cube of n³ blocks (1, 8, 27, 64…). Going from (n−1)² to n² adds an L-shaped border of 2n−1 tiles, so squares are sums of odd numbers: 1+3+5 = 9.

3 · Fibonacci squares and the golden spiral

6 squares

Each new square has a side equal to the two before it added together: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13… Quarter-circles drawn through the squares make a spiral that closely approximates the golden spiral, and the ratio of neighbours approaches φ = (1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.618034.

4 · A seed head on the golden angle

Seed k sits at angle k × 137.5° and radius √k (Vogel's model). The golden angle, 360°(1 − 1/φ) ≈ 137.51°, packs seeds with almost no gaps; nudge the angle and spokes appear. Many sunflowers and daisies show spiral counts that are neighbouring Fibonacci numbers, like 34 and 55, though not every plant does.

5 · A fractal Tree of Life

One rule: every branch splits into two shorter branches (0.72× as long). After depth d there are 2d tips. Real trees aren't exact fractals, but similar repeated branching shows up in trees, rivers, lungs and ferns.

6 · Lambdoma of pure ratios

Tap a cell to hear its ratio over a 220 Hz base.

The lambdoma is a grid of ratios m/n. Row and column numbers 1–8 give every simple fraction: 2/1 is the octave, 3/2 the fifth, 4/3 the fourth. Cells with the same value (1/1, 2/2, 3/3…) lie on one diagonal and sound the same pitch. Tones play softly, starting at 220 Hz × ratio.
