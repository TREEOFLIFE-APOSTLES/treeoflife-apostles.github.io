Tree of Life, drawn by spinning circles

How it works

Any closed curve can be written as a sum of rotating circles: z(t) = Σ ck eikt. Each ck is a Fourier coefficient: its size is the circle's radius, its angle is where the circle starts. Circle k turns k times per lap, riding on the tip of the one before it.

Keeping the N circles with the largest |ck| gives the best N-circle drawing in the least-squares sense (Parseval's identity). Slide from 4 to 64 and watch each rough loop become the symbol.

A shape with holes needs one chain per boundary: the Tree has its outline plus three fruit holes (4 chains), and ∞ takes three, its outer edge and its two holes.
