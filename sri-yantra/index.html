🔺 Sri Yantra · Nine Triangles, One Center

What it is

The Sri Yantra (also written Shri Yantra) is a Hindu sacred diagram — a yantra, a visual aid for meditation and worship. It is associated with the goddess Tripura Sundari, also called Shri. At its center is a single point, the bindu, around which nine interlocking triangles are woven: four pointing up and five pointing down. The triangles sit inside a circle, traditionally inside a square frame with four gates, one in the middle of each side.

Why it is hard to draw

Drawing a Sri Yantra precisely is famously difficult. The nine triangles are not simply stacked: their corners and edges are meant to meet at exact shared points, so moving one triangle even slightly breaks the joins of several others. Traditional makers worked this out by hand over many generations, and simple versions like this page can only suggest the pattern — they are not exact ritual diagrams.

About its age

Yantra worship has deep roots in Hindu and Tantric traditions, and diagrams in this family have been made for many centuries. The exact age of the Sri Yantra in the form we know today is not firmly settled, so this page makes no claim that this precise diagram is a specific number of thousands of years old. What is certain is that people have meditated with it for a very long time, and still do.
