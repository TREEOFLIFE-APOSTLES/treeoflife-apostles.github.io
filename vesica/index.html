🐟 Vesica Piscis · the Fish Window

What it is

The vesica piscis is the almond-shaped lens where two equal circles overlap, each one centered on the other's edge. When each circle's radius is 1, the lens is √3 tall (about 1.732) and 1 wide, and two equilateral triangles fit inside it, sharing the line between the two centers.

Euclid, Elements I.1 (~300 BCE)

The very first proposition of Euclid's Elements builds an equilateral triangle with this same two-circle construction: draw a circle around each end of a line, and where they cross is the third corner.

The Old Babylonian “ox-eye”

Long before Euclid, Old Babylonian scribes drew and measured this same lens shape in their mathematical tablets, calling it the “ox-eye.”

For the Pisces house

“Vesica piscis” means “bladder of a fish.” Two circles meet, and a fish is born in the space they share. Different branches, same tree — a soft window for our Pisces house. 🐟
